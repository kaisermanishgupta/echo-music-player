# Echo Music Player 🎵

A student-built **Android music player application** developed out of curiosity during my **pre-final to final year of B.Tech.**, and later evolved into my **B.Tech. final-year project**.

What started as an exploration into Android development eventually became a publicly released application with users across the world.

## 📱 About

**Echo Music Player** was designed as a feature-rich Android music player with a focus on a clean user experience and practical mobile application development.

The project was independently developed and published on the **Google Play Store around 2019**.

### 🌍 Real-world Reach

The application received a strong response after its release:

* **220,000+ downloads**
* Users from **40+ countries**
* Published and distributed through the **Google Play Store**
* Developed and maintained as a student project

Seeing a project built as a B.Tech. student reach users across different countries was one of the most rewarding outcomes of the project.

## 🎓 B.Tech. Final-Year Project

Echo Music Player began as a **curiosity-driven project during my pre-final year of B.Tech.** and gradually became a substantial application that I carried into my final year.

Unlike a project developed purely as an academic exercise, Echo gave me an opportunity to take an application through the complete journey of:

**Development → Testing → Publishing → Real Users → Maintenance**

The experience introduced me to the practical challenges of building and maintaining software for real users.

## 📊 Fate/What Happened Later?

The application was eventually **delisted from the Google Play Store in 2022**.

Over time, Google introduced newer **privacy, permission, and platform requirements** that required the application to be upgraded. As the project grew older, I was no longer able to dedicate enough time to keep it aligned with the evolving Android and Play Store requirements.

By that stage, my focus had shifted towards **Machine Learning, Deep Learning, professional projects, and GATE preparation**, and Echo was no longer my primary project.

Rather than continuing an application that I could not maintain properly, development was eventually paused.

## 💡 Looking Back

Echo Music Player remains one of my most memorable B.Tech. projects—not only because it became my final-year project, but because it went beyond the classroom and received real-world adoption.

It was an early lesson in:

* Building software from an idea
* Learning a technology independently
* Shipping a product to real users
* Handling production feedback
* Understanding platform and policy changes
* Maintaining software beyond its initial release

The project is preserved here as an archive of that journey.
